# Problem 1 – Potential and energy

For a point charge:

$$
q=4\,\mu C=4\cdot 10^{-6}\,C
$$

The electric potential of a point charge is:

$$
V(r)=k\frac{q}{r}
$$

where

$$
k=9\cdot 10^9 \frac{N m^2}{C^2}
$$

---

## 1. Potential at \(r=0.3\,m\)

$$
V(0.3)=9\cdot 10^9\frac{4\cdot 10^{-6}}{0.3}
$$

$$
V(0.3)=\frac{36\cdot 10^3}{0.3}
$$

$$
V(0.3)=120000\,V
$$

---

## 2. Potential difference between \(0.3\,m\) and \(0.6\,m\)

First:

$$
V(0.6)=9\cdot 10^9\frac{4\cdot 10^{-6}}{0.6}
$$

$$
V(0.6)=60000\,V
$$

Potential difference:

$$
\Delta V=V(0.3)-V(0.6)
$$

$$
\Delta V=120000-60000=60000\,V
$$

---

## 3. Work done to move a test charge

Test charge:

$$
q_0=2\,\mu C=2\cdot 10^{-6}\,C
$$

Work:

$$
W=q_0\Delta V
$$

$$
W=2\cdot 10^{-6}\cdot 60000
$$

$$
W=0.12\,J
$$

---

## 4. Electric field from derivative of potential

The electric field is related to potential by:

$$
E(r)=-\frac{dV}{dr}
$$

Since:

$$
V(r)=k\frac{q}{r}
$$

then:

$$
\frac{dV}{dr}=-k\frac{q}{r^2}
$$

Therefore:

$$
E(r)=k\frac{q}{r^2}
$$

---

## 5. Comparison with Coulomb's law

Coulomb's law gives:

$$
F=k\frac{qq_0}{r^2}
$$

Since:

$$
F=q_0E
$$

then:

$$
E=k\frac{q}{r^2}
$$

This is the same result as obtained from the derivative of potential.

---

## Final results

$$
V(0.3)=120000\,V
$$

$$
V(0.6)=60000\,V
$$

$$
\Delta V=60000\,V
$$

$$
W=0.12\,J
$$

$$
E(r)=k\frac{q}{r^2}
$$

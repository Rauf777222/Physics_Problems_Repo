# Problem 2 – Coulomb's force

Given charges:

$$
q_1=3\,\mu C=3\cdot 10^{-6}C
$$

$$
q_2=-5\,\mu C=-5\cdot 10^{-6}C
$$

Positions:

$$
\vec r_1=(0,0)
$$

$$
\vec r_2=(0.4,0.3)
$$

---

## 1. Distance between charges

Vector from \(q_1\) to \(q_2\):

$$
\vec r=\vec r_2-\vec r_1=(0.4,0.3)
$$

Distance:

$$
r=\sqrt{0.4^2+0.3^2}
$$

$$
r=\sqrt{0.16+0.09}=0.5\,m
$$

---

## 2. Force magnitude

Coulomb's law:

$$
F=k\frac{|q_1q_2|}{r^2}
$$

$$
F=9\cdot 10^9\frac{|3\cdot 10^{-6}\cdot (-5\cdot 10^{-6})|}{0.5^2}
$$

$$
F=9\cdot 10^9\frac{15\cdot 10^{-12}}{0.25}
$$

$$
F=\frac{0.135}{0.25}=0.54\,N
$$

---

## 3. Force vector acting on \(q_2\)

Since the charges have opposite signs, the force is attractive.

So the force on \(q_2\) points from \(q_2\) toward \(q_1\).

Unit vector from \(q_2\) to \(q_1\):

$$
\hat u=\frac{\vec r_1-\vec r_2}{r}
$$

$$
\hat u=\frac{(-0.4,-0.3)}{0.5}=(-0.8,-0.6)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\vec F_2=F\hat u
$$

$$
\vec F_2=0.54(-0.8,-0.6)
$$

$$
\vec F_2=(-0.432,-0.324)\,N
$$

---

## 4. Potential energy of the system

Potential energy:

$$
U=k\frac{q_1q_2}{r}
$$

$$
U=9\cdot 10^9\frac{3\cdot 10^{-6}\cdot (-5\cdot 10^{-6})}{0.5}
$$

$$
U=\frac{-0.135}{0.5}
$$

$$
U=-0.27\,J
$$

---

## 5. Work required to separate the charges to \(2\,m\)

Initial distance:

$$
r_i=0.5\,m
$$

Final distance:

$$
r_f=2\,m
$$

Initial potential energy:

$$
U_i=k\frac{q_1q_2}{r_i}=-0.27\,J
$$

Final potential energy:

$$
U_f=9\cdot 10^9\frac{3\cdot 10^{-6}\cdot (-5\cdot 10^{-6})}{2}
$$

$$
U_f=\frac{-0.135}{2}=-0.0675\,J
$$

Work required:

$$
W=U_f-U_i
$$

$$
W=-0.0675-(-0.27)
$$

$$
W=0.2025\,J
$$

---

## Final results

Distance:

$$
r=0.5\,m
$$

Force magnitude:

$$
F=0.54\,N
$$

Force vector on \(q_2\):

$$
\vec F_2=(-0.432,-0.324)\,N
$$

Potential energy:

$$
U=-0.27\,J
$$

Work to separate to \(2\,m\):

$$
W=0.2025\,J
$$

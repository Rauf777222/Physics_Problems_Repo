# Problem 6 – 2D field map

System: three charges in a plane.

---

## 1. Electric field of one point charge

For a point charge \(q_i\) located at position \(\vec r_i\), the electric field at point \(\vec r=(x,y)\) is:

$$
\vec E_i(\vec r)=kq_i\frac{\vec r-\vec r_i}{|\vec r-\vec r_i|^3}
$$

where

$$
k=9\cdot 10^9\ \frac{Nm^2}{C^2}
$$

---

## 2. Total electric field

For three charges, the total electric field is the vector sum:

$$
\vec E(\vec r)=\vec E_1(\vec r)+\vec E_2(\vec r)+\vec E_3(\vec r)
$$

So:

$$
\vec E(x,y)=
\sum_{i=1}^{3}
kq_i\frac{(x-x_i,\ y-y_i)}
{\left((x-x_i)^2+(y-y_i)^2\right)^{3/2}}
$$

---

## 3. Field map

A field map can be generated by calculating the field vector at many grid points.

For each point:

1. calculate \(\vec E(x,y)\),
2. draw an arrow in the direction of the field,
3. scale the arrow according to the field magnitude.

---

## 4. Equilibrium point

An equilibrium point satisfies:

$$
\vec E(x,y)=0
$$

This means:

$$
E_x(x,y)=0
$$

and

$$
E_y(x,y)=0
$$

In general, this point is found numerically.

---

## 5. Stability of equilibrium

To investigate stability, we displace a test charge slightly from the equilibrium point.

- if the field pushes it back, the equilibrium is stable,
- if the field pushes it away, the equilibrium is unstable.

For electrostatic systems of point charges, stable equilibrium is generally not guaranteed.

---

## 6. Comparison with two charges

For two charges, the field pattern is simpler and usually has symmetry.

For three charges, the field map can be more complex because the field is the vector sum of three contributions.

---

## Final results

Electric field of three charges:

$$
\vec E(x,y)=
\sum_{i=1}^{3}
kq_i\frac{(x-x_i,\ y-y_i)}
{\left((x-x_i)^2+(y-y_i)^2\right)^{3/2}}
$$

Equilibrium condition:

$$
\vec E(x,y)=0
$$

# Problem 7 – Motion in a central field

Given central electric field:

$$
E(r)=k\frac{Q}{r^2}
$$

---

## 1. Force on a charged particle

For a particle with charge \(q\), the electric force is:

$$
\vec F=q\vec E
$$

Because the field is radial:

$$
\vec E(r)=k\frac{Q}{r^2}\hat r
$$

So:

$$
\vec F(r)=qk\frac{Q}{r^2}\hat r
$$

---

## 2. Equation of motion

By Newton's second law:

$$
m\ddot{\vec r}=\vec F
$$

Thus:

$$
m\ddot{\vec r}=qk\frac{Q}{r^2}\hat r
$$

or

$$
\ddot{\vec r}=\frac{kqQ}{m r^2}\hat r
$$

---

## 3. Radial motion

For purely radial motion, the vector equation becomes a one-dimensional radial equation:

$$
m\ddot r=\frac{kqQ}{r^2}
$$

The sign depends on the signs of \(q\) and \(Q\):

- if \(qQ>0\), the force is repulsive,
- if \(qQ<0\), the force is attractive.

---

## 4. Energy

The potential energy of the central electric field is:

$$
U(r)=k\frac{qQ}{r}
$$

The total energy is:

$$
E_{\text{total}}=
\frac12 m v^2+k\frac{qQ}{r}
$$

---

## 5. Positive and negative energy

- Positive total energy usually corresponds to unbound motion.
- Negative total energy may correspond to bound motion for attractive interaction.

This is similar to gravitational motion, where negative energy corresponds to bounded orbits.

---

## 6. Gravitational analogy

The electric central field has the same mathematical form as gravity:

$$
F\sim \frac{1}{r^2}
$$

However:

- gravity is always attractive,
- electric force can be attractive or repulsive.

---

## Final results

Equation of motion:

$$
m\ddot{\vec r}=qk\frac{Q}{r^2}\hat r
$$

Radial equation:

$$
m\ddot r=\frac{kqQ}{r^2}
$$

Potential energy:

$$
U(r)=k\frac{qQ}{r}
$$

Total energy:

$$
E_{\text{total}}=
\frac12 m v^2+k\frac{qQ}{r}
$$

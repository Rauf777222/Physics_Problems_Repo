# Problem 9 – Dipole in an external field

A dipole is placed in a uniform electric field \(\vec E_0\).

---

## 1. Dipole moment

The electric dipole moment is:

$$
\vec p=q\vec d
$$

where \(\vec d\) points from negative charge to positive charge.

---

## 2. Torque on the dipole

The torque acting on a dipole in an external electric field is:

$$
\vec \tau=\vec p\times \vec E_0
$$

Its magnitude is:

$$
\tau=pE_0\sin\theta
$$

where \(\theta\) is the angle between \(\vec p\) and \(\vec E_0\).

---

## 3. Potential energy of the dipole

The potential energy is:

$$
U=-\vec p\cdot \vec E_0
$$

So:

$$
U=-pE_0\cos\theta
$$

---

## 4. Equation of angular motion

For rotational motion:

$$
I\ddot\theta=\tau
$$

The torque tends to align the dipole with the field:

$$
I\ddot\theta=-pE_0\sin\theta
$$

Thus:

$$
I\ddot\theta+pE_0\sin\theta=0
$$

---

## 5. Small displacement approximation

For small angles:

$$
\sin\theta\approx \theta
$$

So:

$$
I\ddot\theta+pE_0\theta=0
$$

This is the equation of a harmonic oscillator.

The angular frequency is:

$$
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{pE_0}{I}}
$$

---

## 6. Physical interpretation

The electric field creates a torque that tries to align the dipole with the field.

For small oscillations, the dipole behaves like a harmonic oscillator.

---

## Final results

Torque:

$$
\vec \tau=\vec p\times \vec E_0
$$

Potential energy:

$$
U=-\vec p\cdot \vec E_0=-pE_0\cos\theta
$$

Equation of angular motion:

$$
I\ddot\theta+pE_0\sin\theta=0
$$

Small-angle equation:

$$
I\ddot\theta+pE_0\theta=0
$$

Angular frequency:

$$
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{pE_0}{I}}
$$

# Problem 5 – Induction in a moving rod

Given:

$$
L=0.25\,m
$$

$$
v=4\,m/s
$$

$$
B=0.6\,T
$$

The rod moves perpendicular to the magnetic field.

---

## 1. Electric field inside the rod

Charges in the rod experience magnetic force:

$$
F_B=qvB
$$

Charge separation creates an electric force:

$$
F_E=qE
$$

At equilibrium:

$$
qE=qvB
$$

So:

$$
E=vB
$$

Substitute:

$$
E=4\cdot 0.6
$$

$$
E=2.4\,V/m
$$

---

## 2. Potential difference between ends

The potential difference is:

$$
U=EL
$$

Since:

$$
E=vB
$$

then:

$$
U=BvL
$$

Substitute:

$$
U=0.6\cdot 4\cdot 0.25
$$

$$
U=0.6\,V
$$

---

## 3. If the motion is not perpendicular to \(\vec B\)

Only the perpendicular component of velocity contributes:

$$
v_\perp=v\sin\theta
$$

Therefore:

$$
U=BLv\sin\theta
$$

If motion is parallel to the magnetic field:

$$
\theta=0
$$

then:

$$
U=0
$$

---

## 4. Dependence on \(L\)

The induced voltage is:

$$
U=BvL
$$

So it is directly proportional to length \(L\).

If \(L\) doubles, \(U\) doubles.

---

## 5. Energy source

The electric field energy comes from mechanical work needed to move the rod through the magnetic field.

The external force does work, and this energy is converted into electrical energy.

---

## Final results

Electric field:

$$
E=vB=2.4\,V/m
$$

Potential difference:

$$
U=BvL=0.6\,V
$$

General case:

$$
U=BLv\sin\theta
$$

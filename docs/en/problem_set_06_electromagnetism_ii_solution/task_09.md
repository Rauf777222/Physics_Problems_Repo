# Problem 9 – Ideal vs. real transformer

---

## 1. Principle of operation

A transformer works using electromagnetic induction.

An alternating current in the primary coil creates a changing magnetic flux in the core.

This changing flux induces voltage in the secondary coil.

---

## 2. Ideal transformer

For an ideal transformer:

$$
\frac{U_2}{U_1}=\frac{N_2}{N_1}
$$

where:

- \(U_1\) is primary voltage,
- \(U_2\) is secondary voltage,
- \(N_1\) is number of turns in the primary coil,
- \(N_2\) is number of turns in the secondary coil.

Power is conserved:

$$
U_1I_1=U_2I_2
$$

Thus:

$$
\frac{I_2}{I_1}=\frac{N_1}{N_2}
$$

---

## 3. Step-up and step-down transformer

If:

$$
N_2>N_1
$$

then:

$$
U_2>U_1
$$

This is a step-up transformer.

If:

$$
N_2<N_1
$$

then:

$$
U_2<U_1
$$

This is a step-down transformer.

---

## 4. Real transformer losses

A real transformer has losses, such as:

- resistance losses in wires,
- eddy current losses in the core,
- hysteresis losses,
- magnetic flux leakage.

Therefore efficiency is less than 100%.

---

## 5. Efficiency

Efficiency is:

$$
\eta=\frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{in}}}
$$

Typical transformer efficiency can be high, often above 90%, but never exactly 100%.

---

## Final results

Voltage ratio:

$$
\frac{U_2}{U_1}=\frac{N_2}{N_1}
$$

Current ratio:

$$
\frac{I_2}{I_1}=\frac{N_1}{N_2}
$$

Efficiency:

$$
\eta=\frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{in}}}
$$

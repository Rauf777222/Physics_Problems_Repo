# Problem 10 – Eddy currents

---

## 1. What are eddy currents?

Eddy currents are circular currents induced in a conductor when it experiences a changing magnetic field.

They appear because of Faraday's law of induction.

---

## 2. Eddy current braking

When a conductor moves through a magnetic field, eddy currents are induced.

According to Lenz's law, the magnetic field created by eddy currents opposes the motion.

This creates a braking force.

---

## 3. Applications

Eddy current braking is used in:

- trains,
- roller coasters,
- electromagnetic brakes,
- damping systems.

---

## 4. Advantages

Advantages:

- no mechanical contact,
- no friction wear,
- smooth braking,
- reliable operation.

---

## 5. Disadvantages

Disadvantages:

- energy is converted into heat,
- braking is weaker at low speeds,
- requires conductive materials,
- can cause unwanted heating.

---

## 6. Best materials

Good conductors generate stronger eddy currents.

Examples:

- copper,
- aluminum.

Ferromagnetic materials can increase magnetic effects, but also introduce hysteresis losses.

---

## 7. How to increase braking force

The braking force can be increased by:

- increasing magnetic field strength,
- increasing conductor speed,
- using better conductive materials,
- increasing conductor thickness or area,
- improving geometry.

---

## Final results

Eddy currents are induced circular currents in conductors.

They oppose changes in magnetic flux and can be used for non-contact braking.

Braking force increases with magnetic field strength, speed, and conductivity.
